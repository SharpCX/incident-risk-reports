02 / Decision First

Executive Summary

The $74 million figure must not be presented as the final loss. At the chain-halt block, the attack position had approximately $120.85 million of oracle-valued borrowings and the risk engine returned an account shortfall of approximately $118.89 million. Separately, roughly $74.31 million of attack-related assets were traceable. The latter may become part of a recovery pool; it is not the liability denominator.

  • [Official] Tectonic said the incident was under investigation and advised users not to interact until safety was confirmed. Root cause, official loss, restart, solvency and legal recovery remained undisclosed.
  • [Official] Cronos Network confirmed a Tectonic exploit and halted the chain.
  • [On-chain] At the halt block, borrowPaused and mintPaused were still false in the affected markets; containment came from the chain halt, not protocol-level market pauses.
  • [Inference] Even if every identified asset were recovered, a mechanical model still leaves about $44.57 million of residual shortfall. This is not a compensation forecast because recapitalization, insurance, other repayments, freezes, legal costs and unidentified assets are excluded.

Pre-incident DefiLlama TVL near $121.7 million is historical context, not current redeemable assets.

04 / Product

Project Background and Strategy

[Official] Tectonic documentation defines the protocol as a Compound-derived money market. Suppliers receive tTokens; redemptions burn tTokens at an exchange rate. Borrowing power is determined by collateral amount, collateral factor and the price oracle.

Yield comes from borrower interest and TONIC incentives. Risk also includes price discovery for thin collateral, oracle updates, liquidation depth, bridges, operational response and legal recovery. Documentation describing twice-hourly or 1%-move oracle updates does not guarantee manipulation resistance in a thin market.

05 / Balance Sheet

Reserves, Liabilities and Transparency

[On-chain] Collateral account 0x2dc6…b618 held tTONIC representing about 326.414 trillion TONIC. At the halt-block oracle price of $0.000000030067, book value was about $9.81 million; after the 20% collateral factor, borrowing capacity was only about $1.96 million.

[Official] No post-incident reserve, insurance, recapitalization, loss-allocation or compensation plan had been published. This report therefore does not calculate a “current coverage ratio.”

07 / Operations

Protocol and Redemption Status

[On-chain] At the halt block, TectonicCore had transferGuardianPaused=false and seizeGuardianPaused=false. Markets used by the attack position had borrowGuardianPaused=false and mintGuardianPaused=false. These are stored contract flags, not evidence that economic safety had returned.

[Official] Tectonic had not published a mint/redeem/withdraw restart schedule, queue, cap or compensation process. Transferability, tToken book value and executable underlying redemption are separate concepts.

08 / Contagion

Full Impact Perimeter

The incident affects several layers that must not be double-counted. The direct loss layer is the main Tectonic pool; suppliers and wrappers hold claims on those same underlying markets. VVS was used as a liquidity path, while the Cronos halt created ecosystem-wide operational disruption without proving asset losses at every dApp.

12 / Analogues

Historical Cases

Historical cases calibrate mechanism and recovery paths; their recovery rates must not be imported into this incident. Mango is the closest manipulation analogue, Euler is an optimistic recovery counterexample, and Moonwell MAMO illustrates coupled oracle/accounting/cap risk.

CaseEvidenceSimilarity / differenceOutcome and lesson
Mango Markets (2022)[Official]Closest mechanism analogue: a thinly traded asset was repriced to inflate protocol borrowing power; product and chain differed.US DOJ cited about $110m and a conviction; legal recovery did not move at the speed of chain containment.
Euler (2023)[Official]Optimistic recovery path through tracing, negotiation and enforcement pressure; Euler was a code exploit, not price manipulation.Euler Foundation said all recoverable funds returned after three weeks; the recovery rate cannot be extrapolated.

13 / Sentiment

Community Sentiment

[Reporting] Visible discussion focused on the $74m versus $119m denominator gap, whether the chain halt preserved assets, and whether restart would involve address restrictions, rollback or special state treatment. These are valid questions, not known answers.

[Community] Claims that rollback was inevitable, all funds were frozen, users would be repaid in full, or final loss was exactly $75m had no official support and were excluded from recovery calculations.

14 / Independent View

Independent Assessment

The central contradiction is not that one figure is “wrong”: the figures answer different questions. $74.31m is the valuation of assets traced to related addresses; $118.89m is the Tectonic risk engine's shortfall for the attack account at the halt block.

Most misleading visible metrics

TVL, tToken exchange rates, stablecoin-pool book balances—and even a possible future badDebt=0 field—cannot independently prove full redemption. Recoverable borrowing and executable cash matter most.

Isolation versus loss

[Inference] The halt kept part of the related assets on Cronos and may improve negotiation, freeze or special-recovery options. It did not erase the attack borrowing from Tectonic's books or restore market cash.

Information that would change the baseline

  • Tectonic post-mortem: root cause, affected contracts and final borrowing ledger.
  • Cronos restart plan: state changes, address restrictions, rollback or simple block production.
  • Whether the LP and ETH are returned, frozen, seized or moved.
  • Loss allocation across reserves, insurance, team capital, protocol token or unsecured supplier claims.
15 / Actions

Risk Actions, Open Questions and Monitoring

Role-specific actions

  • Tectonic suppliers: follow the official no-interaction guidance; preserve wallet and tToken evidence at the halt block; do not treat front-end APY or book balance as redeemability.
  • Cronos protocol managers: treat TONIC/tToken as illiquid and impairable before restart; review local oracle design, collateral factors and supply/borrow caps.
  • Exchanges, bridges and security teams: monitor the listed Cronos/Ethereum addresses; any freeze or return requires lawful authority.

Questions only official or legal processes can answer

  • What is the confirmed root cause and full transaction set?
  • Is the official loss denominator gross borrowing, net outflow, realized bad debt or final supplier loss?
  • Will restart include pauses, oracle replacement, delisting or state changes?
  • What is the ordering of asset return, team capital and user compensation?

16 / Caveats & Sources

Caveats and Sources

  • The official RPC returned 503; PublicNode and dRPC both returned block 90,907,150. The report therefore says verified endpoints showed no later block, not that every validator was monitored.
  • Market reads use the same halt block. accrualBlockNumber can predate the halt in inactive markets; no state-changing transaction forced interest accrual.
  • Ethereum destination value uses the halt-block Tectonic WETH oracle for denominator consistency, not current executable price.
  • No official root cause, final loss, insurance/compensation or legal recovery had been published.
  • Wrappers, LP tokens and their underlying assets are counted once.
