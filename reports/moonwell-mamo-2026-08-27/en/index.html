01

Executive Summary

[On-chain] During the roughly 22-minute rise of the MAMO/USD feed from $0.010597 to $0.431274 (about 40.7×), Base account 0x719e…919D supplied about 14.99M MAMO and successfully established USDC, WETH, wstETH and cbBTC debt across Moonwell Base Core Markets. A 2M AERO borrow call showed outer transaction success but returned a Compound error and emitted no AERO Transfer. At the cutoff, collateral had been almost entirely liquidated and the four remaining debts totaled about $9.124 million; Comptroller account shortfall was also about $9.124 million.

[Official] Moonwell only confirmed an issue in the MAMO Core Market and an investigation. It set all Base Core Market borrow caps and MAMO/WELL supply caps to 1 wei. It had not confirmed an exploit, attacker, root cause, loss, compensation or recovery. Pause flags remained false; the precise description is cap-based containment, not full contract pause.

[On-chain] The incident account then sent 8,729,453.826392 USDC through two CCTP burns to destinationDomain 0. This is gross cross-chain outflow—not Moonwell bad debt or net profit. Acquisition cost, manipulation cost, slippage, liquidation proceeds and address control remain unresolved.

Decision anchor: the most reliable current risk measure is the $9.124 million on-chain shortfall, not the post’s “$6 million net profit” or “$8.7 million stolen.” Visible reserves across the four debt markets were only about $35,900; absent recovery or recapitalization, the reserve-adjusted economic gap was roughly $9.088 million.

02

Scope, Cutoff and Evidence Rules

The report covers Moonwell Base Core Market, MAMO collateral, the incident account and direct market accounting. It excludes unmapped secondary narratives, speculative accomplice addresses and double-counted wrappers. Cutoff: 2026-08-27 21:18:39 CST (UTC+8); chain anchor: Base block 50,523,656 at 21:17:39 CST.

Evidence tiers: [Official] first-party project/governance/regulatory disclosures; [On-chain] reproducible transactions, state and oracle rounds; [Reporting] attributed third-party coverage; [Inference] mechanism analysis; [Community] unverified leads.

Denominators are isolated: supply ≠ protocol NAV; gross borrow ≠ bad debt; account shortfall ≠ executed user haircut; cross-chain gross ≠ attacker net profit; displayed pool liquidity ≠ size-adjusted exit capacity; reserves ≠ legally recoverable assets.

03

Project Background and Strategy

[Official] Moonwell is a Compound-v2-style overcollateralized lending protocol on Base. mToken markets account separately, but the Core Market shares borrowing power through the Comptroller. An overvalued collateral asset can therefore borrow liquid assets from other markets: MAMO price → recognized collateral → multi-asset debt → accounting gaps in affected markets.

MIP-B48 listed MAMO with 50% collateral factor, 30% reserve factor, 110% liquidation incentive, 20M MAMO supply cap and 12M MAMO borrow cap.

[Inference] The design mismatch was not merely whether a price feed existed. It combined a thin, rapidly repriced token, a 50% collateral factor, shared Core borrowing power and a high supply cap. A feed can update as designed while still transmitting an economically manipulable market price.

04

Reserves, Liabilities and Transparency

Moonwell exposes totalSupply, totalBorrows, reserves and cash per market; there is no single protocol reserve that automatically covers every gap. In Compound-v2 accounting, unrepaid borrower debt reduces economic asset value in the affected mToken market until governance recognizes, writes down, recapitalizes or socializes it.

[On-chain] The four remaining debts totaled about $9.124m while visible reserves totaled about $35.9k, only 0.39% of shortfall. Official API data showed USDC cash at zero and WETH/ETH cash near 0.095 ETH. This indicates immediate liquidity exhaustion, not that every deposit was already a legally realized loss.

[Official] No insurance, emergency fund, DAO treasury support, cross-market loss allocation or user recovery plan had been disclosed.

06

Protocol Status, Redemption and Recovery

[Official] Borrow caps across all 21 Base Core Markets were reduced to 1 wei; MAMO and WELL supply caps were also reduced to 1 wei. Other asset supply caps were unchanged. [On-chain] mintGuardianPaused and borrowGuardianPaused remained false for MAMO/WELL, so containment was cap-based.

New borrowing was effectively blocked and new MAMO/WELL supply was capped. Existing deposits, debt, repayment and liquidation depended on each market's state. USDC cash was near zero; WETH, wstETH and cbBTC retained some contract cash, which did not guarantee frictionless withdrawal for every user.

[Official] No second update, ETA, compensation, recovery, legal action or oracle replacement had been published. Status: containment executed; remediation undisclosed.

07

Full Impact Perimeter

  • On-chain account shortfall: about $9.124m—Comptroller collateral deficiency at cutoff prices, not an already-written bad-debt counter.
  • Affected-market reserves: about $35.9k—visible accounting buffer, not total protocol treasury.
  • Reserve-adjusted economic gap: about $9.088m—mechanical assumption that reserves absorb the same-market gap; excludes recovery and recapitalization.
  • Cross-chain gross: 8.729m USDC—face value of two CCTP burns; neither net profit nor one-to-one with shortfall.
  • Final user loss: unknown—depends on freezes, recovery, liquidation, DAO/team/insurance capital and accounting.
  • Legal recovery: unknown—no public confirmation of enforcement, Circle freeze or exchange coordination.

[Reporting] The triggering post described an 8× MAMO move, about $8.7m borrowed and roughly $6m net profit. Chain replay supports the direction but shows a 40.7× feed move from the 17:06 baseline and debt beyond cbBTC/USDC; the $6m net figure lacks full cost attribution.

Price, Liquidity and Liquidation Execution

[On-chain] MAMO/USD rose from $0.010597 to $0.431274 between 17:06 and 17:28 CST, then fell quickly. A later return near $0.009 does not prove the incident-period feed was correct or broken; it proves those rounds were published under the feed mechanism.

Public liquidity was fragmented across Aerodrome and other pools, with the largest individual pools around $0.30m–$0.48m. KyberSwap routes showed increasing size discounts. [Inference] Liquidators faced a large collateral quantity, shallow depth and a rapidly falling feed. Collateral was nearly gone while about $9.124m of shortfall remained.

Exposure and Recovery Scenarios

Recovery sensitivity starts from the $9.124m shortfall and assigns no subjective probabilities.

  • Optimistic — 75%–100% coverage; residual $0–$2.28m: cross-chain assets are frozen/returned or capital covers most of the gap.
  • Observation range — 25%–50%; residual $4.56m–$6.84m: partial recovery or recapitalization, with the rest absorbed over time.
  • Severe — 0%–10%; residual $8.21m–$9.12m: no material recovery or sufficient recapitalization.

Key variables are whether cross-chain funds can be frozen, address control identified, DAO/team willing to recapitalize, accounting treatment, MAMO liquidation residual and additional linked accounts.

Historical Cases

The closest mechanism analogue is Mango: a thin native token entered collateral/position valuation and enabled withdrawal of higher-quality assets. Euler is the important recovery counterexample: successful return can dramatically change final loss, but depends on attacker behavior, enforcement and negotiation.

CaseMechanism similarityKey differenceOutcome / lesson
Mango Markets (2022)Thin token price rapidly inflated; oracle amplified collateral/position value; liquid assets were withdrawn.Mango used perpetual positions; this incident used lending collateral and a shared Core Market.CFTC said oracle price rose over 13× in about 30 minutes and more than $110M was withdrawn; only part was later returned.
Euler (2023)Freeze, tracing, negotiation and a recovery plan followed the exploit.Euler was a code vulnerability, not a thin-collateral price path.All recoverable funds returned; recovery can rewrite final loss but is not a base-case assumption here.
Moonwell cbETH oracle incidentSame protocol’s internal oracle response, post-mortem and governance-upgrade path.Different asset, oracle structure, market state and scale.Useful benchmark for disclosure quality, not for importing a recovery rate.

Community Sentiment and Narrative Risk

[Community] Replies speculated about insiders, team/market-maker-linked addresses and oracle design. No verifiable identity mapping or official conclusion existed at cutoff.

Visible sentiment clustered around objections to thin collateral in a shared market, confusion over an oracle that may function as designed yet remain manipulable, and demands for addresses, bad-debt accounting and compensation. [Inference] Prolonged “investigating” language without reproducible snapshots can turn one incident into a risk-governance credibility problem.

13

Independent Assessment

  1. This looks more like parameter mismatch than a contract exploit. The feed rise is on-chain fact; “oracle hacked” was unproven. A faithful feed can still transmit a manipulable market.
  2. Shared collateral power was the contagion channel. MAMO supported cbBTC, USDC, WETH and wstETH borrowing; AERO failed. Risk should be bounded by borrowable assets and caps, not MAMO TVL.
  3. Emergency caps stopped new risk but came about 71 minutes after the main cross-chain outflow. Monitoring should combine thin-collateral supply spikes, oracle acceleration and multi-market borrowing.
  4. $9.124m is the most useful current anchor, but it changes with interest, asset prices and recovery.
  5. MIP-B48 disclosed liquidity constraints but did not convert executable depth into borrowing limits. Better controls include isolation, dynamic caps, robust TWAP/multi-source pricing and depth-adjusted collateral factors.
14

Risk Actions, Open Questions and Monitoring

Protocol / governance priorities

  • Publish incident addresses, a unified cutoff block and reproducible per-market debt/collateral/reserve/shortfall calculations.
  • Explain feed inputs, frequency, anomaly filtering, OEV wrapper behavior and why the 17:06–17:33 rounds were accepted.
  • Disclose whether Circle, exchanges, law enforcement or cross-chain tracing were engaged.
  • Publish bad-debt recognition, reserve use, treasury/insurance/recapitalization and user recovery treatment.
  • Reassess all thin collateral with isolation, dynamic caps, executable-depth CF and linked supply/borrow circuit breakers.

Open questions

  • What were the source, economic cost and control of the initial 14.99M MAMO?
  • What was the complete cbBTC call path and liquidation recovery?
  • Where did the 8.729m USDC land after destinationDomain 0, and can it be frozen?
  • Are there parallel accounts, MEV/arbitrage or liquidator PnL outside the main address?
  • Does the official root cause involve source data, parameters, monitoring response or several factors?

Caveats and Assumptions

  • The snapshot is point-in-time; interest and prices change USD values.
  • The chain anchor was 21:17:39 CST, Moonwell API 21:18:39 and official-channel search 21:14:39; they are labeled separately.
  • “Reserve-adjusted gap” and market stress rates are scenario accounting, not a governance-approved haircut.
  • CCTP burn proves departure from Base and a destination domain, not final controller, freeze status or legal recoverability.
  • DEX quotes are route- and time-specific; displayed TVL does not replace size-adjusted quotes.
  • Reporting and community claims were not upgraded into fact. Moonwell had not published a root-cause analysis.
